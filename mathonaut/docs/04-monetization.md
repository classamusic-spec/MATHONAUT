# 04 — Monetization

## The structural fact that determines everything

**The player and the buyer are different people.** The child wants spaceships. The
parent wants evidence their kid is learning. You must build for both, and the parent
dashboard is not a feature — **it is the sales pitch**.

## Constraints that kill the lazy models

A kids' app means COPPA / GDPR-K and Apple's Kids Category rules:
**no behavioural ads, no third-party trackers, parental gates on every purchase.**

That rules out:
- **Ad-supported** — prohibited in the form that pays, and a trust-killer besides.
- **Whale-hunting IAP** — you cannot sell currency to a six-year-old, and shouldn't.

This is fine. Neither works in this market anyway.

## Recommended: freemium subscription

### Free tier — genuinely fun, not a demo
- Verdant Nebula + Amber Drift (levels 1–5)
- Daily Flight
- A few cosmetics, first 2 crew
- Full offline play

If the free tier feels like a paywall trailer, reviews punish you and word of mouth
dies. It has to be a real game.

### Mathonaut Academy — paid
- All galaxies, full 13+ level curriculum
- **Parent dashboard** — per-skill progress, facts mastered/shaky, practice minutes
- Multiple child profiles
- Full crew, hangar, sticker logbook
- Weekly progress email

## Simpler alternative for launch: premium one-time

**$4.99, no accounts, no backend.**

- Lower LTV, but: parents love it, reviews are better, zero compliance surface for
  accounts, and it validates demand before you build subscription infrastructure.
- Migrate later with "founders keep lifetime access" — this is a well-trodden path
  and it buys enormous goodwill.

**Recommendation:** if the team is small, ship premium first. Prove retention. Then
build the subscription with real data about which content people actually finish.

---

## The long game: schools

Prodigy built a 100M-user business because kids demanded at home what they played at
school. That is the real ceiling for a maths app.

- Teacher dashboard, class codes, printable reports, curriculum alignment
  (Common Core / UK National Curriculum)
- Requires student-privacy work (FERPA in the US, plus district procurement)
- **Phase 3.** Do not start here.

---

## Never do

| | Why |
|---|---|
| Ads of any kind | Prohibited / trust-killer |
| Purchasable currency aimed at the child | Prohibited in Kids Category; predatory |
| Paid loot boxes | Regulatory landmine + headline risk |
| Pay-to-win perks | Breaks the game and the trust |
| Countdown/pressure timers, streak guilt | Dark patterns aimed at children |
| Social comparison between children | Unsafe and demotivating for the ones who need it most |

Earned random cosmetics (cargo capsules) **are** fine — no money touches them.

## Honest market read

This space is crowded and **Khan Academy Kids is free and excellent**. You will not
win on features.

**Real differentiation:**
1. *"The maths is the steering, never a quiz popup."* Almost every competitor is a
   quiz with a game bolted on. This is a game where the answer is a flight path.
   That is a genuine, demonstrable difference in 5 seconds of video.
2. The adaptive ladder that starts at **number recognition age 4** and runs to 12×
   tables — most apps pick one narrow band.
3. Production values. This looks like a game, not courseware.

**Success will hinge more on distribution than features:**
- Apple Kids Category editorial (achievable — they like polished, ad-free, ethical)
- Teacher word of mouth
- Parent social (the sticker logbook / postcards exist partly for this)
- App Store Optimisation on "math game for kids", "learn addition"

**Metrics that matter:** D1/D7 retention, sessions/week, trial→paid conversion,
and the one that actually predicts renewal — *measured accuracy improvement per child*.
If the dashboard can't show a real gain, parents cancel. Which is why `02-curriculum.md`
matters more than anything in this doc.
